How to use ChatGPT for writing quotes and estimates

Short answerFeed ChatGPT your pricing once, then paste a plain-English job and it drafts a clean line-item estimate you edit and send. The trick is setup: give it your real labor rate, your markups, and one of your past estimates as a template, so it writes in your numbers, not made-up ones. Never send what it produces without checking the math.

How to do it

1

Put your real numbers in first, or it will guess

Start a ChatGPT Project (a workspace that keeps files and instructions in one place; free tier holds 5 files, paid up to 40). Upload one past estimate and a short note with your burdened labor rate and your markups. Without this it invents prices, and invented prices are worse than no estimate.

2

Give it your burdened labor rate, not your wage

A tech who costs $30/hour on paper really costs about $45 once you add payroll tax (7.65%), workers' comp and benefits (~15%), non-billable time (~10%), and vehicle cost. Tell ChatGPT the real burdened number. If you feed it the wage, every quote is underpriced by a third.

3

Set your markups so it applies them every time

Materials usually carry 15 to 25 percent markup; labor and sub labor 25 to 50 percent. Tell it which you use. Also tell it markup is not margin: a 20 percent markup is only a 16.7 percent margin, so on a $10,000 job that is $2,000 profit, not $2,400.

4

Paste the job in plain English

Type it the way you'd say it: 'replace 40 gallon water heater, add expansion tank, haul the old one.' Ask for a line-item estimate with materials, labor hours, and your markups applied. It returns a structured draft in seconds.

5

Add the parts that protect you

Make sure the draft includes scope, exclusions (permits, hazardous material, owner-supplied items), a 5 to 10 percent contingency line, payment terms, an expiration date (15 to 30 days), and how they approve it. These are what stop a cheap job from becoming a fight later.

6

Check the math, then send

ChatGPT is fast at structure and confident at arithmetic it sometimes gets wrong. Re-add the line items yourself before it goes out. Treat it as a fast junior estimator, not the final word.

Where this goes wrong

The parts most guides skip, and the ones that actually cost you money:

  • Trusting its prices. If you did not give it your rates, the numbers are made up. It will still present them confidently. Always feed your real pricing first.
  • Skipping the math check. Language models transpose and round wrong. The structure will look perfect while a total is off. Re-total every estimate before sending.
  • Confusing markup with margin. Pricing off markup and thinking it is margin quietly eats your profit on every job. A 20 percent markup is a 16.7 percent margin.
  • No exclusions line. The estimate that does not say what is NOT included is the one that turns into an argument. Make ChatGPT include exclusions every time.
  • Pasting customer data you shouldn't. Anything you upload can be retained. Keep sensitive customer details out unless you are on a plan and settings you have checked.

Common questions

Do I need the paid version of ChatGPT for this?

No. The free tier can draft estimates. The paid Projects workspace just holds more files (40 vs 5) and keeps your pricing template handy, which saves setup time if you quote daily.

Will it know my local prices?

Only if you tell it. It has no idea what lumber or a service call costs in your area. Give it your real numbers once and it will reuse them; skip that and it guesses.

Is this better than estimating software like Joist or Housecall Pro?

Different tool. Software is better at storing price books and turning estimates into invoices. ChatGPT is better at turning a messy description into a first draft fast. Many owners use both.

Can it send the estimate to the customer for me?

No. It drafts the text. Sending, e-signature, and getting paid still happen in your invoicing tool or email. If you want the whole flow automated, that is a build, not a prompt.
