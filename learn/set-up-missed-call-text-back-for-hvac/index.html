How to set up a missed-call text-back for HVAC companies

Short answerWhen an HVAC call goes unanswered, an automatic text hits the caller in under a minute so they stop dialing your competitor. Google Voice cannot do this; you need a tool that can. Pick a provider, set it to text every missed call, write one short message under 160 characters, and test it by calling your own number.

How to do it

1

Know why this matters before you build it

78 percent of buyers go with the first company that responds, and leads reached within 5 minutes are 21 times more likely to become customers than ones reached after 30. On a hot July day when three units die at once, the shop that texts back first books the work. That is the whole point of this.

2

Do not try to do it with Google Voice

Google Voice has no auto-reply, in the free or the Workspace version, and no automations or third-party integrations. If you are on Google Voice you send every text by hand, which defeats the purpose. You need a tool built for this.

3

Pick a tool that does missed-call text-back

Standalone SMS tools and field-service phone systems have it built in, roughly $99 to $300 a month depending on the tool. Port your existing number or use a virtual one. The feature is common now; you are choosing on price and whether it fits the rest of your stack.

4

Set the trigger: every missed call, or after-hours only

Decide when it fires. Most HVAC shops want it on every missed call during the day (you are on a roof, hands full) and separately after hours. Set the ring threshold so it only triggers after a real miss, not when you pick up on the fourth ring.

5

Write one message, under 160 characters

Include your business name, a quick 'sorry we missed you,' what to do next, and a timeline. Example: 'Hi, this is [Company]. Sorry we missed your call, we're on a job. Text what you need and we'll call back within 30 min.' After hours, add an URGENT keyword for real emergencies.

6

Test it by calling your own number

Before you trust it, call your business line, let it miss, and confirm the text lands within about 60 seconds. Texts get a 98 percent open rate and most are read within three minutes, so a working setup genuinely catches callers who would otherwise be gone. 85 percent of missed callers never call back.

Where this goes wrong

The parts most guides skip, and the ones that actually cost you money:

  • Relying on voicemail. Most people see 'missed call,' skip the voicemail, and dial the next shop. The text is what keeps them with you.
  • Using Google Voice for it. It cannot auto-reply. If that is your phone tool, this feature is not available and you need a different one.
  • A long or robotic message. Over 160 characters gets cut off. Keep it short, human, and clear on what happens next.
  • No after-hours version. Half your missed calls happen when you are closed. Set a separate after-hours message with an emergency path or you lose the night calls.
  • Never testing it. A silent, broken auto-text feels like it is working while it catches nobody. Call your own number and confirm the text actually arrives.

Common questions

Can I do this for free with Google Voice?

No. Google Voice has no auto-reply feature in any version. You need a tool built for missed-call text-back, which typically runs $99 to $300 a month.

How fast does the text go out?

A working setup sends within about 30 to 60 seconds of the missed call. That speed is the point, because the caller is still deciding who to hire.

What should the message say?

Under 160 characters: your name, 'sorry we missed you,' text us what you need, and when you will get back to them. Add an URGENT keyword for after-hours emergencies.

Is this worth it for a small shop?

If missed calls cost you jobs, yes. 85 percent of missed callers never call back, and text gets a 98 percent open rate. One recovered install usually pays for a year of the tool.
