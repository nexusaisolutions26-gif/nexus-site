AI for HVAC companies: scheduling and dispatch

Short answerAI scheduling assistants can assign a job to the right tech based on skill, location and availability, and text the customer an arrival window without anyone touching it. That is real and it works. What it will not do is replace the dispatcher's judgment on a bad day, so treat it as the default that a human overrides.

How to do it

1

Fix reminders before you touch routing

Automated SMS reminders cut no-shows by roughly 70 percent. No-shows are the most expensive hole in an HVAC schedule because the truck still rolled. Close that first; it takes minutes.

2

Let the software pick the tech

AI scheduling in tools like Jobber and Housecall Pro assigns jobs by skill, location and availability, and pushes real-time arrival updates to the customer. On a heat-wave day with a full board, that beats a human guessing.

3

Group jobs by location, not by call order

Reducing drive time between jobs is where the hours come back. Cluster by neighborhood instead of dispatching in the order calls came in, and you fit more calls per day with the same trucks.

4

Automate the customer updates

Booking confirmation, day-before reminder, and a 'tech is on the way' text should all fire on their own. This is the single biggest driver of a smooth day, and it removes the phone tag that eats your office person.

5

Match the tool to your crew size

Solo or two trucks: Jobber, roughly $29 to $49/month. Two to five: Housecall Pro, about $79/month with GPS dispatch and online booking. Five to twenty techs: ServiceTitan, roughly $200 to $400/month. Do not buy the enterprise tool for a two-truck shop.

6

Keep a human on the exceptions

AI handles the normal day well. Emergency calls, an angry customer, a tech running two hours behind: those still need a person to re-cut the board. Use the automation as the default, not the decider.

Where this goes wrong

The parts most guides skip, and the ones that actually cost you money:

  • Automating dispatch before fixing no-shows. A perfectly routed day still loses money if nobody is home. Reminders first.
  • Dispatching in call order. It feels fair and it wastes hours in the truck. Group by location.
  • Manual 'on the way' texts. They get skipped on the busiest days, which are exactly the days they matter most.
  • Buying enterprise software too early. ServiceTitan is built for 5 to 20 techs. Below that you are paying for a dispatcher you do not have.
  • Trusting AI on emergencies. Priority calls and unhappy customers need human judgment. Keep an override and use it.

Common questions

Can AI really assign jobs to the right technician?

Yes. AI scheduling assistants in Jobber and Housecall Pro assign based on skill, location and availability, and send customers real-time arrival estimates. It is a well-established feature, not a promise.

What does it cost for a small HVAC shop?

About $29 to $49/month for a solo operator (Jobber), around $79/month for a small crew (Housecall Pro), and roughly $200 to $400/month for ServiceTitan at 5 to 20 techs.

What is the fastest win?

Automated SMS reminders. They cut no-shows by about 70 percent and take almost no setup.

Will this replace my dispatcher?

No. It replaces the routine assignment work and the reminder texts. Emergencies, re-cuts, and upset customers still need a person.
