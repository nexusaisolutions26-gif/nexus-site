AI for roofing companies: writing quotes and estimates

Short answerAerial and photo-based measurement tools turn a roof estimate into a few minutes of desk work instead of a site visit, and crews that stop driving to every measurement go from 3 to 5 estimates a day up to 6 to 10. Start there, then automate the follow-up on estimates you already sent. A human still has to sanity-check every scope.

How to do it

1

Kill the unnecessary site visit first

Aerial measurement is the fastest win in roofing. Crews that stop driving out for every measurement move from 3 to 5 estimates a day to 6 to 10. Roofr charges about $19 per report with no monthly fee, so you can test it on the next ten leads without committing to software.

2

Sanity-check every AI scope before it goes out

Photo and aerial tools produce a workable scope in minutes, but the scope still needs a human to check it against what the inspector actually saw. Missed layers, hidden decking damage, and access problems do not show up in a satellite image. Never send an unreviewed scope.

3

Automate follow-up on estimates you already sent

This is the most underrated category in roofing. Operators call automatic follow-up the difference between leads that quietly go cold and leads that convert weeks later. One company that finally tracked its leads recovered $14,000 in the first month, not from new marketing, just from following up on leads they had already paid for.

4

Answer the phone during storm season

Speed decides who gets the job. Leads contacted within 5 minutes are far more likely to convert than ones contacted after 30. If you cannot answer during a storm surge, an automated response holding the lead is worth more than a perfect estimate delivered two days late.

5

Add routing only when you are running multiple crews

AI-assisted routing replaces the whiteboard when you have several crews across a wide territory. Its returns are clearest for larger operations. If you run one or two crews, skip it for now; the payback is not there yet.

6

Sequence it so you see results this week

The recommended order is aerial measurement, then ChatGPT or Claude for writing, then review automation. That stack is free or nearly free, takes under 3 hours total to set up, and shows a measurable change in estimate turnaround inside a week.

Where this goes wrong

The parts most guides skip, and the ones that actually cost you money:

  • Sending an AI scope unreviewed. The measurement is reliable; the scope judgment is not. No AI tool climbs a ladder, and it cannot see what your inspector saw.
  • Buying a full platform before fixing measurement. GoHighLevel runs about $97/month base plus usage. Roofr is $19 per report. Prove the workflow before you take on a monthly bill.
  • Chasing new leads while old estimates rot. The cheapest revenue in roofing is the estimate you already sent and never followed up on.
  • Buying routing software for one crew. Routing pays off across multiple crews and wide territories. Below that it is overhead.
  • Assuming adoption means it is easy. Larger companies adopt roughly twice as often as small ones, mostly because they have someone with time to implement. Budget the setup hours honestly.

Common questions

How much faster are AI roof estimates really?

Fast enough to change your day. Crews that switch to aerial measurement go from 3 to 5 estimates daily up to 6 to 10, mostly by not driving to every measurement.

What does it cost to start?

Roofr is around $19 per report with no monthly fee, so a ten-lead test costs under $200. GoHighLevel is about $97/month base plus usage if you want the fuller stack later.

Can AI write the whole estimate?

It can produce a workable scope in minutes, but it must be checked against the inspector's findings before it goes to the homeowner. Treat it as a fast first draft.

What is the highest-return automation if I only do one?

Follow-up on estimates already sent. One roofing company recovered $14,000 in a month purely from following up on leads they had already generated and lost track of.
