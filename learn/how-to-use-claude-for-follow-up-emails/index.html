How to use Claude to write follow-up emails

Short answerDo not paste a fresh prompt every time. Make one Claude Project for follow-ups, put your real past emails and your price list in its knowledge base, and write project instructions that describe your voice and your rules. After that a follow-up takes one line of input. Anthropic's own prompting guidance says to include 3 to 5 examples for best results, and that is exactly what makes the difference between output that sounds like you and output that sounds like a chatbot.

How to do it

1

Collect five follow-ups you actually sent and that actually worked

Real ones, out of your sent folder. Quotes that went quiet, a job you finished last month, a customer who asked for a price and disappeared. Strip the names. These five are worth more than any prompt template you will find online, because they are the only record of how you really talk.

2

Create a Project instead of a chat

Projects are self-contained workspaces with their own chat history and knowledge base. Free accounts get up to five, which is enough. Name it something like Follow-ups. Everything you upload stays available to every chat inside it, so you stop re-explaining your business.

3

Upload the knowledge base

Your five sample emails, your service and price list, your service area, and a one-page note on what you will and will not do. On Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise, Claude switches to retrieval when the material gets large, which Anthropic describes as expanding capacity by up to 10x. On free, keep it tight.

4

Write project instructions that read like rules, not like a personality

Be blunt. 'Write at a sixth grade reading level. Four sentences maximum. Never apologize for following up. Never invent a price. If you need a number I have not given you, leave a bracket. No exclamation points.' Rules get followed. Vague adjectives like professional and friendly do nothing.

5

Know which setting to reach for

Claude has three layers. Profile instructions live under your initials then Settings then Instructions for Claude and apply everywhere. Project instructions apply only inside that project. Styles adjust tone and format of the response itself. Put your business rules in project instructions, not in your profile, or every unrelated chat starts writing like a plumber.

6

Feed it one line and let it do the work

'Quote 3140, $2,850 water heater swap, sent Tuesday, no reply, homeowner mentioned they were getting one other bid.' That is the whole input. You built the context once so you never type it again.

7

Ask for three versions, then take the shortest

Request a soft nudge, a direct one, and a close-the-file one. The short direct version wins more often than owners expect. Anything over five sentences reads like a sales sequence, and people can tell.

8

Save the winner as a Gmail template

Turn on Templates under Settings then See all settings then Advanced, then save the version you keep reusing from More options then Templates in the compose window. Templates are desktop only. Now the routine version costs you zero seconds and Claude only handles the odd ones.

Where this goes wrong

The parts most guides skip, and the ones that actually cost you money:

  • It will invent a price if you leave room for one. Put the real numbers in the knowledge base and say in your instructions to use brackets for anything missing. A follow-up quoting a price you never agreed to is a real problem, not a typo.
  • Profile instructions bleed into everything. Owners put 'write like an HVAC contractor' in the account-wide instructions, then wonder why an email to their accountant reads like a service call. Keep business voice in the project.
  • Follow-ups do not fix a bad quote. If your quote had no scope, no timeline and no next step, no email rescues it. Silence after a quote is usually a quote problem. Fix the document and the follow-ups get shorter.
  • Five identical examples teach it a rut. Anthropic's guidance asks for examples that are relevant, diverse, and structured. If all five of your samples are the same polite nudge, that is the only email you will ever get back.

Common questions

Do I need a paid Claude plan?

No. Projects are available on free accounts, capped at five. Paid plans add retrieval for larger knowledge bases and team sharing. For a follow-up project holding a price list and five emails, free is genuinely enough.

How many follow-ups before I stop?

Three, then let it go, and say so in the third one. A stated last email gets more replies than a fourth unstated one, and it keeps you out of the spam complaint category.

Should Claude send the emails too?

Not at first. Draft, read, send yourself for a month. You will catch the two or three assumptions it keeps making wrong, and those go into the project instructions. Automate after that, not before.

Claude or ChatGPT for this?

Either handles it. What matters is that you set up a persistent workspace with your real examples in it instead of retyping context. The tool with the workspace you actually maintain is the better tool.
